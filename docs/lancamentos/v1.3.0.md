Retro Computer 1.3.0 spreads the desktop over two monitors on Windows and Mac, with a taskbar on each screen, and lets you organize the Browser's tabs and favorites by dragging.

## New

- **Both screens in full screen** (Windows and Mac): Control Panel › Appearance › Settings › **Use both screens in full screen** makes the desktop cover every monitor, even ones of different resolutions. The icons stay on the main screen; each screen shows the whole wallpaper; windows go from one screen to the other by dragging, and maximize and snap on the screen they are on; menus never cross to the other screen; a video in full screen fills the monitor under the pointer. On Windows, with monitors at different scales (a laptop at 150% and a monitor at 100%), Retro Computer reopens once to measure both screens in real pixels. On a Mac, the window covers both monitors only with "Displays have separate Spaces" turned off in System Settings › Desktop & Dock (and after logging out and back in); the Control Panel says so.
- **A taskbar on every screen**, on by default: the one on the main screen keeps every window, the pinned programs, the search and the tray; the one on each other screen has Start, the buttons of the windows on that screen and the clock. Right-click the taskbar › **Show the taskbar on all displays**, or Settings › Taskbar and Start Menu, turns it off. Right-clicking the background of the other screen opens the desktop menu.
- **Browser tabs in any order**: drag a tab along the tab bar, like the favorites.
- **Favorites folders**: right-click the favorites bar › **New folder…**; drag a favorite onto a folder to put it inside, or use **Move to**. A folder on the bar opens as a menu, with **Open all in tabs**. On the home page, folders are tiles too.
- **Home page tiles in any order**: drag the big favorite icons on the Browser's home page to rearrange them, or onto a folder tile.

## Fixed

- The night light now stays on over videos and pages in full screen.
