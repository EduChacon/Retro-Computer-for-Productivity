Retro Computer 2.0.0 brings a PDF Reader that highlights passages and adds notes, a spell checker that works offline, internet calendars in the Calendar, the cloud on Linux, and a lock screen that locks with your Linux password when you close the laptop lid.

## New

- **PDF Reader** (Start › Programs › Accessories; double-clicking a PDF opens it): the pages, text selection and copying, fit to width, actual size and go to page. **Highlight** paints the selected text in yellow, green, blue or pink, and **Note** places a sticky note with your comment wherever you click (drag to move it, double-click to edit it). The list of marks takes you to each one; right-click changes the color, edits or deletes, and Ctrl+Z undoes. **Save** writes the marks into the PDF itself as standard PDF annotations, which other PDF readers on computers and phones also show, and keeps the previous file in Previous versions; **Save As…** saves a copy. Only highlights and notes are ever removed: the PDF's other annotations and forms stay as they were. A PDF with a digital signature asks first, because saving over it would invalidate the signature (the signed version is still kept).
- **Spell checker** for text fields, in Portuguese, English and Spanish, with the Hunspell dictionaries shipped with Retro Computer (nothing is downloaded). Right-clicking a misspelled word shows the suggestions and **Add to dictionary**, also in the Craft programs. Control Panel › Language › Spelling turns it off or chooses the languages (on macOS, the system's own checker is used).
- **Internet calendars** in the Calendar (File › Internet calendars): subscribe to a calendar by its iCal address (`https://` or `webcal://`), such as Google Calendar's secret address, a published Outlook calendar, holidays or a school calendar. Their events appear with a globe, read-only, with the details and **Copy to Calendar**; reminders are optional for each one. The calendar is downloaded again every hour without cookies, and a copy works offline. The address stays only in this computer's settings and never appears on screen.
- **The cloud on Linux** (Start › Programs › Accessories › Cloud): sign in to Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox in the browser, and the account appears in Computer and in the folder bar as a drive, through rclone, a free program that the Cloud window offers to install (with FUSE, which shows the cloud as a folder). As with USB drives, files are brought in and taken out as copies; nothing in the cloud is deleted or overwritten from Retro Computer.
- **Locking with the Linux password** (Retro Computer as the system): even without a Retro Computer password, the screen now locks with your Linux sign-in password when you close the lid (also when the lid is set not to suspend), before the computer sleeps, when it wakes up and when you leave the screen saver. The computer only goes to sleep after the lock screen has appeared, so the desktop is never visible for a moment when you open the lid. This needs a Linux account with a password; each moment can be turned off in Control Panel › Passwords.

## Fixed

- **White lines inside letters** at the larger pixel sizes on Linux (1.25×, 1.5×, 2.5×…): the light hinting that Linux Mint uses by default snapped each row of dots to the screen grid on its own. The pixel fonts now have one outline per shape, with exactly the same dots; at whole sizes the screen is identical.
- **A gap under maximized Linux programs** (such as Firefox) above the taskbar: the space reserved for the taskbar was fixed, and now follows its real height (with the date in the clock, the pixel size and the Mac theme).
- With Retro Computer as the system on Linux Mint, the window could stay at 1280 × 800 in the middle of the screen: Openbox is now a dependency of the `.deb`, the session waits for it, and without a window manager the window covers the screen by itself.
- After closing a dialog, focus goes back to the last window used (not the last one opened), and a dialog opened from another one always stays on top.

## Changed

- PDFs now open in the PDF Reader; the other programs that open PDFs stay in **Open with**, and **Open with › Choose program…** changes the default.
- With Retro Computer as the system, the screen saver asks for the password by default (Control Panel › Passwords).
